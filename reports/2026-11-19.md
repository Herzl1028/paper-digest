### 1. Jailbreak Detection on Large Language Models via Contrastive Prototype Structures

**中文标题**: Jailbreak Detection on Large Language Models via Contrastive Prototype Structures

**作者**: Yan Qiu, Yuanyuan Li

**中文摘要**: Jailbreak attempts and harmful prompts remain a challenge for the safe deployment of large language models. We study whether contrastively learned representations can support safety detection without training an additional downstream classifier. We propose Jailbreak Detection with Prototype Inference (JDPI), which uses binary safety labels to construct harmful and benign prototypes and makes predictions from relative prototype distances. JDPI uses only binary safety labels for encoder adaptation, prototype construction, and validation. In our setup, it uses fewer labeled examples than the classifier baseline (Method A) by avoiding the separate split used to train the downstream classifier. Across four benchmarks covering different input formats, contrastive fine-tuning improves JDPI's detection accuracy by 9.1-18.0 percentage points. Using the same encoder, JDPI achieves slightly higher ROC-AUC on one benchmark and stays within 0.017 of the baseline across all four. With dataset-specific adaptation, JDPI exceeds the released Llama Guard 3-8B baseline by 0.173-0.310 in ROC-AUC across the four benchmarks. In multi-turn replay on TomGibbs, JDPI uses accumulated user history as the conversation unfolds, achieving higher conversation-level accuracy than when it uses the current turn alone. Across 12 directed transfers, JDPI outperforms the classifier by an average of 1.59 percentage points in accuracy, demonstrating stronger cross-dataset robustness.

### 2. Knowing What You Know: A Trait Theory of Reviewer Value in Human-AI Cognitive Systems

**中文标题**: Knowing What You Know: A Trait Theory of Reviewer Value in Human-AI Cognitive Systems

**作者**: Priyanka Sinha, Sugam Pandey

**中文摘要**: When a capable model and a human reviewer cooperate on the same decision, which human still adds value? We argue this is a question about a single cognitive trait, metacognitive calibration: the alignment between a reviewer's behavioral confidence and the actual correctness of their judgments. We develop a trait theory of reviewer value to make it precise. The theory makes five claims: value is governed by calibration rather than by activity, speed, agreement, or tenure; calibration leaves observable traces in behavioral telemetry; those traces are non-diagnostic on their own, because the fast, low-revision signature of a calibrated expert is indistinguishable from that of a confident novice until it is gated by outcomes; human calibration and a model's self-knowledge are the same construct, so the joint system should defer to whichever agent is better calibrated on each item; and individual calibration is causally identifiable only above the level of the single judgment. We ground each claim in a six-month production window of an AI-assisted accounting system (541621 reviewer events, 483 reviewers), and find that a purely behavioral expertise profile inverts the ground truth, scoring an overconfident cohort (5.2 percent correction survival) higher than genuine experts (99.2 percent); that reviewer quality is flat across tenure, so the trait is not learned on the job; and that the same calibration logic that separates good from bad reviewers, applied to the model, concentrates 92.5 percent of human effort on genuine errors. We close with the boundary the theory must respect: under majority-vote aggregation, individual causal value is unidentifiable.

### 3. Mapping the AI Supply Chain: Dependencies, Vulnerabilities, and Market Concentration in the AI Ecosystem

**中文标题**: Mapping the AI Supply Chain: Dependencies, Vulnerabilities, and Market Concentration in the AI Ecosystem

**作者**: Sarah H. Cen, Lindsey A. Gailmard, Joohan Kim

**中文摘要**: AI systems are built across many organizations that contribute models, data, compute, capital, talent, and other assets. While the layers of the AI stack are relatively well understood, the relationships between actors and components across layers remains sparsely documented. In this work, we focus on the organizations that make up the AI supply chain: the complex network of actors that contribute to the development and deployment of AI. We motivate the examination of the organizational AI supply chain---highlighting concerns about concentration, power, chokepoints, cascading failures, fragmented accountability, and risk---then describe our pipeline for mapping the AI supply chain. Our pipeline yields our main contribution: a multilayered, large-scale dataset of actors and relationships in the AI ecosystem, annotated with dates, descriptions, types, tags, and more. This work takes a step toward systematically mapping and measuring the AI supply chain and its properties, cataloging how models, data, chips, infrastructure, capital, and even talent flow between organizations. This data enable critical examinations of industry relationships and yield insights into the political economy of AI development and concentration. We provide several illustrative analyses, including studying the most central actors, analyzing network concentration, and identifying communities and chokepoints. While our dataset is fairly comprehensive, it is also necessarily incomplete: public documents omit private deals, and coverage is shaped by search design. Together, the approach, data, and findings aim to serve as a resource for studying the AI ecosystem. The dataset is publicly available at https://huggingface.co/datasets/sarahcen/ai-supply-chain and can be explored interactively at https://aisupplychains.org/.

### 4. Agentic Harness Engineering Beta: Requirement-Centered Evolution of Agent Harnesses

**中文标题**: Agentic Harness Engineering Beta: Requirement-Centered Evolution of Agent Harnesses

**作者**: Jiahang Lin, Jiahao Chen, Yiding Wang

**中文摘要**: Real-world harness engineering is driven by explicit and implicit user requirements, which guide development and validation. However, existing harness evolution methods often overlook implicit requirements and interaction needs, while using a single evolution agent to modify the harness and benchmark scores to select changes. This process often produces narrow patches and leads to benchmark-specific local optima. We introduce Agentic Harness Engineering Beta (AHE-Beta), a requirement-centered loop that brings real-world engineering practices into harness evolution. The loop consists of three steps: (1) User-driven evaluation lets simulated users raise their requirements as issues while they work with the harness over multiple turns. (2) Team-based modification implements those requirements as generalizable changes rather than task-specific patches. (3) Requirement-driven selection merges a change only when it resolves a verified requirement and passes review. Empirically, on held-out tasks, AHE-Beta raises pass@1 from 21.9% to 33.3% while using 40.8% fewer tokens than Base Harness, and achieves higher pass@1 than Claude Code, Meta-Harness, and AHE. In a blind human A/B test, real users prefer AHE-Beta in 31 of 48 valid pairs and rate it higher on all five dimensions, matching the directional preference of the user simulator. The human A/B results show that AHE-Beta achieves its goal of aligning harness evolution with real-world user requirements. Without further evolution, the gains transfer to GLM-5.3 and DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash with pass@1 improvements of 15.8% and 5.3% over Base Harness. Together, these results show that requirement-centered harness evolution is a more general method for improving agent capabilities.

### 5. Unified Law for Visual Tasks (The Four Laws of Computer Vision)

**中文标题**: Unified Law for Visual Tasks (The Four Laws of Computer Vision)

**作者**: Sen Fang, Yalin Feng, Yancheng Zhang

**中文摘要**: In this paper, we propose a Unified Law for Visual Tasks, which could be further divided into Four Laws of Computer Vision, with the goal of enabling a single framework or method to uniformly train on and perform nearly all visual tasks. Our starting point is an inspiration and hypothesis: if the world information is encoded into image, which we call World Snapshots, then image processing is world processing, and image generation is World Snapshot generation. Furthermore, if all visual and visualization tasks can be reformulated as generation tasks, then a unified method, World Snapshot Models (WSMs), can perform the vast majority of visual tasks, much as large language models have largely unified natural language processing. This paper validates these laws across more than 1,000 trained and evaluated visual tasks (defined by distinct input-output mappings) and presents a unified training framework for future visual-task research and applications.
